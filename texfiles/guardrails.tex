\section{Guardrails}\label{sec:guardrails}

Guardrails are the central abstraction enabling performance introspection. 
A guardrail comprises: (i) a performance property that should hold for a policy or subsystem and can be checked at run-time, and (ii) a corrective action to take when that check fails.
% Informally, a guardrail augments a policy with a run-time check and a corresponding response. The check captures a condition that should continue to hold while the policy is operating, and the response specifies what the system should do when that condition fails.
% In this way, guardrails make explicit a part of policy design that is often left implicit today: it requires developers to explicitly list what assumptions justify the decision logic used in a policy, and how the system should react when those assumptions no longer hold.
This abstraction is intentionally separate from the policy: guardrails do not replace the policy, but equip it with a mechanism for detecting and responding to deployment-time performance failure modes.
% The policy continues to make decisions in the usual way, whether through hand-written heuristics, profiled cost models, or learned predictors.
% The rest of this section develops this abstraction further.
We first distill the design principles that guide useful guardrails in \Cref{subsec:design-principles}, and then use these principles to design a domain-specific language (DSL), \lang, for expressing guardrails.
